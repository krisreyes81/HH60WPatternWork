Vector sticks

Pick an aircraft, press Draw, then click points on the map. Drag the white handle in the middle of a stick to curve it. Pull it back to the middle to make it straight. Drag an end point to move it. Click a Jack to get the option to commit: the aircraft slows, stops there for the time you set, then flies on. Click any end point to add or change a commit. Flown sticks clear off the map. Press Play to fly it. Before you click, small text by the cursor shows the distance to the nearest threat.

Formation

In ground track mode, trail flies the same path lead flew and holds the spacing above, measured along the track it is about to fly. Editing trail on its own takes it out of formation. Tick the box again to rejoin.

Saved setups

A setup keeps the background, map image, terrain, wind, chart turn rate choice, threats, Jacks, both aircraft (position, heading, airspeed), spacing, sticks, and patterns as they are right now.

Nothing saved yet

Setups are kept in this browser on this device. To move one to another device or keep a backup, copy its code and paste it there. A code does not carry the map image, so load the picture again on the other device.

↶ Undo on the map takes back your last action and puts the sim back to the moment just before it. + Pattern in the Map tool bar lets you pick a pattern type and adds it, using the sizes set in the Gunnery pattern box. On a touch screen, pinch to zoom and drag with two fingers to move the map. Hide controls gives the map the whole screen and puts the map tools on its left edge. There, ☰ All tools opens any section of the controls right on the map. Keys: 7 8 9 0 1 2 set 70 to 120 kt, − and = change airspeed by 5, M move map, S sticks, R measure, E erase, T trail follows lead, Esc closes a section. Use ◀ 1 s and 1 s ▶ on the map to step back or forward one second at a time. The Left and Right arrow keys do the same, and Space plays or pauses. You can go back up to 15 minutes.

You can drag aircraft, threats, Jacks, and patterns in every tool except Measure and Terrain.

Wind pushes the aircraft. On sticks or a pattern they crab into the wind to hold the line. Aircraft in formation are drawn larger than scale when zoomed out.
